# On Attention

The hardest part of thinking is not the thinking itself but holding still long enough for a thought to arrive. Distraction is rarely loud. It is almost always polite, small, well-intended — a little interruption wearing a useful face.

I have been reading old essays on the discipline of writing, and one line has kept returning to me: *attention is the beginning of devotion*. I suspect the reverse is also true — that what we attend to, we come to love, and what we love, we finally understand.

## What the tools are for

A good writing surface should `disappear`. It should feel closer to a sheet of paper than a screen. When the interface fades, what remains is the voice.

> "We write to know what we think. The page is the mirror."

This is why I keep coming back to markdown — not because it is the fastest or the most powerful format, but because it refuses to get in the way. Ceremony is the enemy of clarity.

## A small practice

Each morning I open a blank page and write three sentences before I read anything. No inbox, no feed, no plan. The rule is only that the sentences must be true. Some mornings they are dull; most mornings they are enough to start.

> [!note]
> The point is not the sentences. The point is the ten quiet minutes that produce them.

Attention, it turns out, is not something you find. It is something you build — one unhurried page at a time.
